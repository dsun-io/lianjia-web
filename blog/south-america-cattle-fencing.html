Cattle Fencing for Brazil & Argentina

LJ Anping Lianjia Metal Products Co., Ltd.
| August 28, 2026 | 7 min read | Reviewed by Factory QC | Regional Guide

Contents

Brazil and Argentina run some of the largest cattle operations on earth, and their ranches fence enormous areas — the pampas, the cerrado, the pantanal. A fence that fails on a property like that is not a repair job, it is a herd-management problem. The products that dominate this market are hinge-joint field fence (牛栏网), ring-lock where security matters more, and Y posts to carry the line. Here is how to spec them.

At a glance

  • Wire: 1.8–2.5 mm hinge-joint line wire, 2.5 mm edge wire — the standard for cattle.
  • Coating: hot-dip zinc 80–120 g/m² for 10–15 years outdoors; heavier grades for wet or coastal areas.
  • Roll weight: e.g. 7-90-30 (200 m) ≈ 60 kg in 2.0/2.5 mm wire — weight drives freight cost.
  • Support: pair the net with hot-dip galvanized Y posts (star pickets).

Why South American ranches fence this way

Two things shape the choice of fence in Brazil and Argentina: scale and climate. Properties are measured in hundreds or thousands of hectares, so fencing cost per metre decides how much of the ranch actually gets fenced. At the same time, regions range from the humid cerrado to drier pampas, with thunderstorms and strong sun over large areas. That is why the market standard is hinge-joint netting in hot-dip galvanized wire — strong enough for cattle, priced per metre at a level that makes fencing a whole property feasible, and durable enough to stand up for a decade or more.

Hinge joint vs ring lock

For cattle, hinge-joint (拧编) is the traditional and most widely used weave: the horizontal line wires twist around the vertical stays, producing a rectangular mesh that is tighter at the bottom and wider near the top. It is strong, flexible, and handles cattle pushing on it well. Ring-lock (环扣) locks every intersection with a separate ring, giving a flatter, stiffer mesh — it is used where a more rigid, secure fence is required, such as for deer, wildlife, or high-value paddocks. For most beef operations, hinge joint is the right starting point.

Wire and mesh specs

A typical cattle spec for this market looks like this:

Parameter Typical range
Line wire diameter 1.8 – 2.5 mm
Edge wire 2.5 mm (common upgrade)
Tensile strength 350 – 550 MPa
Mesh Tight bottom, wider top (e.g. 7 line wires, 90 cm high, 30 cm spacing)
Roll width / length 0.5–2.0 m wide; 30–50 m rolls (heavier spec capped at ~30 m for handling)

The mesh opening matters: too wide and calves slip through, too tight and the fence is unnecessarily heavy and expensive. A common all-round choice is 7-90-30 — 7 line wires, 90 cm high, 30 cm vertical spacing — which holds mature cattle while staying economical.

Zinc coating for the climate

Corrosion, not mechanical damage, is the main reason field fence fails in this region. The practical benchmark is hot-dip galvanizing at 80–120 g/m², which gives roughly 10–15 years of outdoor life in most ranching areas. In wet, high-rainfall or coastal regions, upgrade to a heavier coating (for example 200–230 g/m²) to extend the service life significantly. Beware thin electro-galvanized wire — it looks similar at first and rusts in a season or two, which on a big property is an expensive lesson. Put the coating weight on the purchase order and verify it before shipment.

Roll weights & freight

Because ranches buy in volume, freight often costs more than the fence itself, and freight is charged by weight. Roll weight is therefore a spec you should always confirm. As a guide:

Specification Approx. weight / roll
7-90-30, 200 m, 2.0 / 2.5 mm wire ≈ 60 kg
7-90-30 HD, 200 m, all 2.5 mm wire ≈ 85 kg
10-120-15, 100 m, 2.0 / 2.5 mm wire ≈ 55 kg
13-115-15, 100 m, all 2.5 mm wire ≈ 95 kg

Weight depends on wire diameter, mesh and length. Heavier spec (thicker wire, tighter mesh, higher fence) uses more steel and is heavier per roll — compare total landed cost, not just the unit price.

Posts & support

Netting alone does not hold cattle. The line needs posts at intervals, with strain posts at corners and gates. Y posts (star pickets) made from high-strength steel are the common choice — they drive into the ground, take the netting clips, and are fast to install across long runs. Specify hot-dip galvanized posts and match the post length to your mesh height. For the heaviest lines or wet ground, concrete-set steel posts at corners and ends are standard practice.

How to order for Brazil and Argentina

To quote accurately, send: knot type (hinge joint or ring lock), line/edge wire diameters, mesh configuration (line wires × height × spacing), roll length, zinc coating weight, and your destination port. We supply hinge-joint and ring-lock field fence in all the standard configurations above, plus Y posts and accessories, and we confirm roll weight and coating before shipment.
